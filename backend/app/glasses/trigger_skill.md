# Setting triggers

A trigger is a short caption. A matching model compares it with each still as a whole picture
and gives a score. The model matches what things look like; it cannot reason, count, read or
remember. So the trigger finds the moment, and you do the thinking when you are woken.

**Cast wide, then filter.** A trigger you did not set can never wake you, and a missed moment
cannot be got back. A false alarm costs one look at a still, and you can filter it out for good.
So set many triggers at first, and narrow down as you learn what this place looks like.

## 1. From the goal to triggers

Ask: at the moment the person needs me, what object will be in front of them?

| The goal is about | Trigger on | You decide when woken |
|---|---|---|
| A thing ("tell me when my bus comes") | the thing: "a city bus" | whether it is the right one (read its number) |
| A place or a moment ("before I leave home") | an object that marks it: "a front door" | whether the moment has come, from the still and your notes |
| A risk ("I am allergic to nuts") | what carries the risk: "a food package held in a hand" | whether the risk is real (read the label) |
| A step in a task ("glasses on before grinding") | the tool or part that starts the step: "an angle grinder" | whether the step was done right |

## 2. Wording

- One object per trigger, worded as a caption of a photo of it: article, look, noun. "A green
  watering can", "a white pill box".
- Say what it looks like, not what it is for or whose it is.
- Add colour, shape or material only when the object really has a look of its own.
- Start with six to ten triggers: the thing itself in its different looks, its parts, and what
  is usually seen with it or just before it. For road works: a traffic cone, a road barrier, an
  excavator, a worker in a hi-vis vest, a road works sign, a dug-up road.
- Filter as you go. A trigger that keeps waking you for nothing gets a higher level, then is
  dropped. A trigger that found the real thing is kept, and tells you which other looks to add.
- When you are woken and see something you had not thought of that belongs to the goal, add a
  trigger for it.

## 3. What the matching model cannot do

Leave these to yourself, when you are woken:

- **Places, actions and states:** "a kitchen", "leaving", "the door is open".
- **Details:** a colour, a brand or a type alone hardly changes the score.
- **Reading:** numbers, names, labels, prices, speeds.
- **Things always in view:** they match every still and tell you nothing.
- **Order and time:** "again", "on the way back", "for the second time". Keep these in your
  notes.

## 4. False alarm or real sighting

Each time you are woken, first decide which of the two it is. They are handled differently, and
never both ways at once.

**False alarm: the thing is not in the still.** The match was wrong, so raise the detection
floor: set that trigger's level to just above the score that woke you. Do not snooze it; a
snooze would hide the real thing as well.

**Real sighting that needs no action: the thing is in the still, but there is nothing to say
now.** The match was right, so leave the level where it is; raising it would make you miss the
thing next time. Snooze the trigger instead, for as long as the thing is likely to stay in
view, and note what you are waiting for.

| What happened | Level | Snooze |
|---|---|---|
| New trigger | The low level you are told | No |
| False alarm | Raise to just above the score that woke you | No |
| The same trigger's third false alarm | Drop the trigger; the others cover the goal | No |
| Real sighting, wrong moment or nothing to say | Leave it | Yes, while it is likely to stay in view |
| Real sighting, you spoke, and the person asked for every time | Leave it | Yes, so you do not repeat yourself about the same one |
| Real sighting, you gave the reminder | The goal is done: answer with no triggers | No |

Small or distant things score weakly. Keep their level low and expect look-alikes.

## 5. One reminder

Remind once. When you have said what the goal asked for, the goal is done: answer with no
triggers, even if the thing stays in view or comes back. Keep going after speaking only when
the person asked for it in words: "every time", "each", "whenever", "keep telling me".

## 6. When you are woken

1. Look for the thing itself in the still. Not there: a false alarm, raise the level. There:
   a real sighting, go on.
2. Check what the matching model could not: the detail, the text, the moment.
3. Speak only if it helps the person now, in one short sentence. Check your notes first: if
   you have already given this reminder, stay quiet.
4. Write your note: one line on what you saw and what you are waiting for. It is your only
   memory.
